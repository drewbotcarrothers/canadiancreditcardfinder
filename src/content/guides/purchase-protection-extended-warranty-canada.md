**Purchase protection (also called purchase security) covers eligible new items you buy with your card if they're stolen or accidentally damaged soon after purchase, typically within 90 days. Extended warranty insurance adds time to the manufacturer's original warranty, typically up to one extra year.** Both come built into many Canadian credit cards, including plenty with no annual fee.

## How purchase protection works

If an eligible item you bought with your card is stolen or damaged within the coverage window, the insurer will repair it, replace it or refund you, up to the limits in the certificate. Typical features:

- **A 90-day window.** Simplii, TD, BMO and Amex all describe coverage for 90 days from the purchase date on several of their cards.
- **A per-claim limit.** American Express, for example, lists up to $1,000 per occurrence on the [Amex Green Card](/card/american-express-green-card/) and the [Cobalt](/card/american-express-cobalt-card/).
- **A lifetime limit.** BMO's certificates on cards like the [BMO CashBack Mastercard](/card/bmo-cashback-mastercard/) list a $60,000 lifetime maximum.
- **Coverage is usually excess insurance.** If you have home or tenant insurance, you may need to claim there first, with the card covering what's left.

## How extended warranty works

Extended warranty insurance usually **doubles the manufacturer's original warranty, up to one additional year**. So:

- A product with a 1-year manufacturer's warranty gets 1 more year, for 2 years total.
- A product with a 6-month warranty gets 6 more months.
- A product with a 3-year warranty usually gets just 1 more year, since the extension is capped.

Certificates usually require the original warranty to be valid in Canada, and some cap how long the original warranty can be. Simplii, CIBC, BMO and Scotiabank all describe their extended protection as doubling the original warranty up to one additional year.

## What's usually excluded

Each certificate has its own list, but common exclusions include:

- Items lost or simply misplaced (as opposed to stolen)
- Cash, gift cards, tickets and similar
- Used, refurbished or second-hand items (often excluded, though some certificates cover refurbished items with a warranty)
- Motor vehicles and their parts
- Damage from normal wear and tear, or from misuse
- Items left unattended in a public place

Phones are often excluded from extended warranty when the card has separate mobile device insurance. See our guide to [mobile device insurance](/guides/mobile-device-insurance-credit-cards-canada/).

## How to make a claim

1. **Pay for the full item with the card.** Some certificates allow points or a split payment, but check the wording first.
2. **Keep the paperwork:** itemized receipt, card statement, original warranty, and a police report for theft.
3. **Call the insurer quickly.** Certificates set a deadline to report a claim, often within a short window after the incident.
4. **Don't throw out the damaged item** until the insurer says so. They may ask to see it.

## FAQ

**Do I need to register my purchases?**
Generally no. Coverage applies automatically when you pay with an eligible card.

**Is purchase protection the same as price protection?**
No. Price protection, which a few cards offer, refunds the difference if an item's price drops. Purchase protection covers theft and damage.

**Do authorized users get the coverage too?**
Usually, when they pay with their own card on your account. Check the certificate.
