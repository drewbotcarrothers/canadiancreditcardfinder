**Choose [American Express Cobalt](/card/american-express-cobalt-card/) if restaurants, cafés, bars, food delivery, and stand-alone grocery stores dominate and that combined spend stays inside Cobalt’s $2,500 monthly 5x cap. Choose [American Express Gold Rewards](/card/american-express-gold-rewards-card/) if you want uncapped 2x on Canadian groceries, gas, and drugstores, a $100 annual travel credit, or limited lounge access.** Both earn Membership Rewards. Cobalt does not include lounge access. Gold does, in a smaller package than Platinum: four complimentary Plaza Premium visits a year in Canada, and a Priority Pass membership whose visits are charged. Fees and welcome offers change — confirm them on the live card pages, not from memory.

This is a Canada-only comparison. U.S. Gold and U.S. Cobalt are different products with different credits and networks. If you only fly Air Canada and think in Aeroplan, also open the [American Express Aeroplan Card](/card/american-express-aeroplan-card/) before you assume Membership Rewards is the right currency.

## The short answer

Cobalt is priced as a monthly membership and pays 5x on eligible eats and drinks in Canada: restaurants, bars, cafés, food delivery, stand-alone grocery stores, and delivery of food and groceries, up to a combined $2,500 in net purchases a month, then 1x. Gold Rewards bills a yearly fee and pays 2x on eligible travel plus uncapped 2x on eligible gas, grocery, and drugstore spend in Canada. Dining on Gold is ordinary 1x. Grocery on Cobalt is 5x only at stand-alone grocery stores and grocery delivery, and only inside that shared monthly cap with restaurants and delivery. That is the whole argument, once you ignore the colour of the plastic.

Households that eat out often *and* shop a supermarket every week sometimes hold both, especially once the combined eats-and-drinks bill clears $2,500. Add the two fees first. If the combined membership is larger than the extra points you will actually redeem toward travel, one card plus a [no-annual-fee cash-back card](/guides/best-no-annual-fee-credit-cards-canada/) is cleaner.

## How Membership Rewards actually pays you

Both cards dump points into the same Canadian Membership Rewards pool. That is useful if you later add [The Platinum Card](/card/the-platinum-card/) — you are not splitting currencies. It is less useful if every redemption is an Air Canada flight reward. Transferring Membership Rewards to Aeroplan can work, but a cobrand that *earns* Aeroplan skips the conversion step.

Redeeming as a statement credit is the simple door and usually the weaker cents-per-point. Transferring to airline or hotel partners is the stretch door. If you will only ever cash out, a cash-back Amex such as [SimplyCash Preferred](/card/simplycash-preferred-card-from-american-express/) may be less work than learning partner charts.

Category codes follow American Express, not your receipt. A hot counter inside a big-box store, a restaurant inside a hotel, a pharmacy counter inside a grocer, or a warehouse club can miss the bonus bucket. Stand-alone grocery stores are in Cobalt’s 5x eats-and-drinks category. Costco Canada does not take American Express. Keep a Visa or Mastercard for the till that declines Amex — that is still a real constraint in smaller towns and some independent grocers.

Canadian Amex personal cards typically do not add a separate foreign-transaction fee on the issuer side. Merchant conversion can still move the total. Confirm the cardmember agreement before a trip.

## Cobalt: the dining card

Cobalt exists because 5x on eligible eats and drinks in Canada is still one of the strongest rates in the country. American Express includes stand-alone grocery stores in Canada, and delivery of food and groceries, in that 5x bucket, together with restaurants, bars, cafés, and food delivery. The cap is a combined $2,500 in net purchases per month; after that, those purchases earn 1x. Our Cobalt review also describes 3x on eligible streaming and 2x on eligible gas, transit, and ride share, with 1x on everything else, including drugstores. Eligible hotel and car-rental bookings through Amex Travel Online earn an extra Membership Rewards point, for 2x total. Confirm those multipliers on the live page — they are the reason to pay the monthly fee.

The welcome offer in our data is not a single lump after one spend hurdle. You typically earn a slice of points in each monthly billing period where you hit a purchase threshold, up to a first-year cap. Miss a quiet month and that slice is gone. Current or former Cobalt cardmembers are usually excluded. Read the eligibility line before you apply for a “second” bonus.

Cobalt is a strong supermarket card up to the cap. Stand-alone grocery stores and grocery delivery share the $2,500 monthly 5x bucket with restaurants and delivery. If that combined bill is usually higher, Gold Rewards’ uncapped 2x grocery rate, or a grocery cash-back Visa, can beat the 1x that applies above the cap. Drugstores are outside Cobalt’s 5x bucket. See [best grocery credit cards in Canada](/guides/best-grocery-credit-cards-canada/) if the supermarket is the real decision and the cap will not cover it.

Additional Cobalt cards currently list no extra annual cost in our data, which helps a household put more restaurant bills on the same product. Confirm that on the application.

## Gold Rewards: the household earn card

Gold Rewards is the mid-tier Membership Rewards card: uncapped 2x on grocery, gas, and drugstore, a $100 CAD annual travel credit, limited lounge access, and a fee that is easier to justify if those 2x categories run all year. The travel credit applies to a single Amex Travel Online booking of at least $100. Lounge access is four complimentary Plaza Premium lounge visits per calendar year in Canada, plus a Priority Pass membership with the annual membership fee waived and lounge visits charged at prevailing rates. A $50 NEXUS statement credit is available every four years. Eligible travel — flights, hotels, car rentals, cruises, and similar — earns two points per dollar in our current review. Eligible hotel or car-rental bookings through Amex Travel Online can add another point on top. Dining is 1x. Platinum remains the card with the Global Lounge Collection and Fine Hotels + Resorts.

The first additional Gold Rewards card is a meaningful household perk in our data. Further extras cost. Couples who will both put groceries and gas on Amex should price that structure against two Cobalt cards (often $0 extra) or one Gold plus a no-fee Visa.

Welcome-bonus value in our Gold data has often been large relative to the annual fee for *new* cardmembers who complete the offer. Current or former Gold Rewards cardmembers are typically ineligible. The detailed unlock text is sometimes thin on the sheet — write down the spend hurdle and deadline from the issuer page. Year one can look attractive on paper; year two is only the fee versus ongoing 2x earn.

Gold is a limited lounge card, and it is still a mid-tier card. The four Plaza Premium visits and paid Priority Pass access are a Canadian package, separate from the Global Lounge Collection. If you want that collection and a broader set of travel credits, read [The Platinum Card](/card/the-platinum-card/) and [whether premium fees are worth it](/guides/are-premium-credit-cards-worth-it-canada/).

## A simple way to choose without inventing a spreadsheet

1. Pull three months of statements. Split spend into dining/delivery, grocery, gas/drugstore, travel bookings, and everything else.
2. Ignore interest. If you carry a balance, neither card is the product — see [low-interest versus rewards](/guides/low-interest-vs-rewards-credit-cards-canada/).
3. Apply Cobalt’s 5x to dining, delivery, and stand-alone grocery only up to a combined $2,500 a month, then 1x. Apply Gold’s 2x to grocery, gas, drugstore, and travel with no eats-and-drinks cap. Treat everything else as 1x on both. Count Gold’s $100 travel credit and four Plaza Premium visits only if you will use them.
4. Subtract each card’s current yearly cost from the live card page. Cobalt’s monthly fee times twelve is the yearly cost.
5. If one card wins by a wide margin, stop. If they are close, prefer the card whose bonus category you cannot easily move to a $0 Visa. Cobalt wins heavy dining and grocery inside the cap. Gold wins uncapped grocery, gas, and drugstore, plus the travel credit and limited lounge.

You do not need a precise cents-per-point. You need to know whether eats and drinks under $2,500, or uncapped grocery plus gas and drugstore, is the larger wallet. Most households already know.

## When a third Amex is the honest answer

**Platinum** is a travel-membership invoice: lounge access, credits you have to remember to book, and 2x on dining and travel. It is a weak grocery card. Do not buy Platinum to “win” the Cobalt-versus-Gold debate.

**Aeroplan Card** is for people who already book Air Canada and want Aeroplan, not a flexible bank currency. Grocery coverage on Amex Aeroplan products is often weaker than Visa cobrands. If supermarket acceptance is the constraint, a [TD Aeroplan Visa Infinite](/card/td-aeroplan-visa-infinite-card/) may fit better than any Amex.

**SimplyCash Preferred** is the cash-back Amex: capped grocery/gas cash back and a 2% catch-all, billed as a monthly fee. Choose it if you will not transfer points and you already live on the Amex network.

Browse the full [American Express lineup](/issuer/american-express/) before you apply for two products in the same week. Amex Canada welcome-bonus rules are strict about current and former cardmembers.

## Merchant acceptance and the backup card

American Express acceptance in Canada has improved at chains and online checkouts. It is still not Visa or Mastercard. Independent restaurants — ironically Cobalt’s best category — are the most likely to decline Amex in smaller cities. Grocer banners vary. You need a backup network card regardless of which Amex you pick.

A practical pair for many households:

- Cobalt or Gold for the bonus categories that take Amex
- A $0 Mastercard such as [Tangerine Money-Back](/card/tangerine-money-back-credit-card/) for the rest, including grocers that skip Amex

That pair is often cheaper than two paid Amex cards and still covers the till.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating “grocery” as the same category on both cards.** On Cobalt, stand-alone grocery stores and grocery delivery sit in the 5x eats-and-drinks bucket with restaurants, up to $2,500 a month combined, then 1x. On Gold, grocery, gas, and drugstore are a separate uncapped 2x category, and restaurants stay at 1x.
- **Chasing the larger welcome headline.** Gold’s first-year bonus can look bigger; Cobalt’s offer is paced monthly. Neither bonus is available if you already held the card.
- **Ignoring the second-year fee.** Welcome math is a first-year story. Cancel or downgrade if year-two earn does not cover the membership.
- **Using either card as a borrowing product.** Purchase rates on rewards Amex cards will erase a 5x dining rate in one cycle.
