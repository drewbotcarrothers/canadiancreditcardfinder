**A declined credit card application doesn't go on your credit report as a "decline," but the hard inquiry from the application does. The most useful thing you can do is figure out why it happened before you apply anywhere else.** Firing off more applications right away adds more inquiries and rarely fixes the underlying problem.

## The most common reasons applications are declined

**1. Income below the card's minimum.** Visa Infinite and World Elite cards have published income requirements. For example, the [TD Cash Back Visa Infinite](/card/td-cash-back-visa-infinite-card/) lists $60,000 personal or $100,000 household income, and the [BMO Ascend World Elite Mastercard](/card/bmo-ascend-world-elite-mastercard/) lists $80,000 individual or $150,000 household. Applying with less is a common reason for a quick decline. Our guide to [Visa Infinite and World Elite income requirements](/guides/visa-infinite-world-elite-income-requirements-canada/) explains the tiers.

**2. A thin or new credit file.** If you have little or no Canadian credit history, the issuer has nothing to judge you on. This is common for students, young adults and newcomers.

**3. Too many recent applications.** Several hard inquiries in a short period can signal financial stress to lenders.

**4. High balances relative to your limits.** If your existing cards are close to maxed out, a new lender may see more risk.

**5. Missed payments, collections or a past bankruptcy.** Some issuers spell this out. BMO's eligibility text, for example, asks that you have not declared bankruptcy in the past seven years.

**6. Residency or status requirements.** Some cards require Canadian citizenship or permanent residence. BMO's cash back cards, for example, list "Canadian citizen or permanent resident" as a requirement.

**7. Errors.** A typo in your income, address or date of birth, or an error on your credit report, can cause a decline.

## Step 1: Read the letter, then call

Issuers usually send a letter or email after a decline. It may name the reason, or the credit bureau they used. If it's vague, call the issuer and ask:

- What was the main reason for the decision?
- Which credit bureau did you check?
- Would I qualify for a different card from you, such as a no-fee or student card?

Some issuers will look at an application again if you can clear up a detail, such as correcting your income or adding household income. It doesn't hurt to ask.

## Step 2: Check your credit report for free

Both Equifax and TransUnion let you request your credit report for free, and checking your own report is a soft inquiry that doesn't affect your score. Look for:

- Accounts you don't recognize
- Late payments that you believe were on time
- Old debts that should have dropped off
- Incorrect personal details

If you find a mistake, you can dispute it directly with the bureau. Our [credit scores guide](/guides/how-credit-scores-work-canada/) explains what's on your report and how long items stay there.

## Step 3: Wait, and fix the cause

There's no official waiting period, but a few months between applications gives inquiries time to matter less, and gives you time to make real changes:

- Pay every bill on time, every month.
- Pay down balances so you're using less of your available credit.
- If your file is thin, keep one card open and use it lightly, paying in full.

## What not to do

- **Don't apply for several cards in one week** hoping one will stick. Each application is a hard inquiry.
- **Don't inflate your income.** Issuers can ask for proof, and misstating income on a credit application can have serious consequences.
- **Don't close your oldest card** to "clean up" your file. A longer history generally helps.

## FAQ

**Does a declined application hurt my credit score?**
The decline itself isn't reported, but the hard inquiry from applying is. One inquiry has a small effect; several in a short time can matter more.

**How long should I wait to apply again?**
There's no rule, but waiting a few months and fixing the reason for the decline gives you a better chance than reapplying right away.

**Can I ask the bank to reconsider?**
You can call and ask. If the decline came from a detail you can clarify, like income, some issuers will review it.
