**Getting approved for a credit card and qualifying for its welcome bonus are two different things. Each Canadian issuer writes eligibility rules into its offer terms, and they range from "once per card, ever" at American Express to "no Scotiabank personal card in the last two years" at Scotiabank.** If you read only the headline, you can end up paying an annual fee for a card whose bonus you were never going to get.

This guide summarizes what each major issuer's offer terms said when we checked them in October 2026, explains the common patterns, and gives you a checklist to run before you apply. Offers change often, and the terms attached to the specific offer you apply under are what count. Always read the footnotes on the live card page.

## Quick summary by issuer

| Issuer | What the offer terms typically exclude |
| --- | --- |
| American Express | Current or former cardmembers of that specific card |
| Scotiabank | Anyone who held any Scotiabank personal credit card, as primary or secondary cardholder, in the past 2 years, including product switches |
| TD | Customers who opened (or activated or closed) the same card family in the last 12 months; Aeroplan's own rule also applies to Aeroplan cards |
| CIBC | Anyone who opened, transferred or cancelled another card in the same family (Aventura, Dividend, Aeroplan) within the last 12 months; product changes |
| BMO | Current or former holders of that card who reopen or reinstate it during the offer period; existing BMO cardholders who switch into it |
| RBC | Existing RBC personal cardholders who transfer into the card; additional cardholders |

The details follow.

## American Express: once per card

Amex's Canadian card pages use short, consistent wording. On the [American Express Cobalt Card](/card/american-express-cobalt-card/) page, for example: "Current or former Cardmembers with this Card are not eligible for this offer." The Gold Rewards Card page uses the same language, and other Amex cards carry similar wording.

The rule has no time limit. If you have ever held that particular card, assume you won't get its welcome bonus again. That is why people call it a "lifetime" rule, though it is applied card by card rather than across Amex as a whole: holding a Cobalt doesn't stop you earning a Gold Rewards bonus under the wording above.

Two more points:

- **Aeroplan's terms also apply** to Amex Aeroplan cards. Their offers note that Aeroplan's own terms on previously received welcome or incentive points apply too (more on that below).
- **Amex bonuses are often spread out.** The Cobalt offer pays points for each month in your first year in which you hit a spending threshold, so cancelling early leaves part of the bonus behind.

## Scotiabank: the two-year rule

Scotiabank's rule is the broadest in Canada. The offer terms on the [Scotiabank Gold American Express Card](/card/scotiabank-gold-american-express-card/) page say: "Individuals who are currently or were previously primary or secondary cardholders of a Scotiabank personal credit card in the past 2 years, including those that switch from an existing Scotiabank personal credit card, as well as employees of Scotiabank, are not eligible for this offer." The same wording appears on other Scotiabank personal cards, such as the Scotiabank Passport Visa Infinite.

What that means in practice:

- **Any** Scotiabank personal credit card counts, not just the same product.
- Being a **secondary (supplementary) cardholder** on someone else's Scotiabank card counts too.
- Switching an existing Scotiabank card to a new one doesn't earn the new card's bonus.

If you want a Scotiabank card for its welcome offer, plan it as your first Scotiabank card in two years.

## TD: 12 months on the same card family

TD's offer terms are product-based. The TD First Class Travel Visa Infinite offer, for example, says it "is not available to customers who have activated and/or closed" that card account in the last 12 months, and the TD Cash Back Visa Infinite page uses the same wording for its own product.

For Aeroplan cards, the [TD Aeroplan Visa Infinite](/card/td-aeroplan-visa-infinite-card/) terms require that you "not have opened any TD-Aeroplan Visa Card account (e.g., Platinum, Infinite, Infinite Privilege or Business) in the last 12 months." On top of that, TD notes that Aeroplan's general terms allow bonus points to be withheld or revoked if you have previously received welcome points on a new Aeroplan credit card "with TD or another issuer of the same card category type."

## CIBC: 12 months, and cancelling counts

CIBC's terms, such as those for the [CIBC Aventura Visa Infinite](/card/cibc-aventura-visa-infinite-card/), say CIBC may approve your application but you are not eligible for the offer "if you have opened, transferred or cancelled another Aventura card within the last 12 months." CIBC applies the same family-based idea to its Dividend and Aeroplan cards. Offers apply to newly approved accounts, and transfers or product changes from other CIBC cards are excluded.

Note the word **cancelled**. Closing a CIBC Aventura card resets the clock just as opening one does.

CIBC Aeroplan cards also fall under Aeroplan's "same card category type" rule described above.

## BMO: no reopening, no switching in

BMO's [offer terms page](https://www.bmo.com/popups/main/personal/credit-cards/terms-and-conditions-en.html) uses wording like this for the BMO eclipse Visa Infinite: "This offer is not available to current or former BMO eclipse Visa Infinite cardholders who reinstate a closed account or open a new account for the same card during the Offer Period. Existing BMO credit card cardholders who transfer into this product during the Offer Period are not eligible for this offer."

So with BMO:

- Product switches into the card are excluded.
- Former holders of the same card who reopen it or apply again during the offer period are excluded.
- BMO offers have defined application windows, so the "offer period" matters.

BMO's terms also say bonus points may be cancelled if the card is closed within 365 days of opening, on several of its points cards. Plan to keep the card at least a year.

## RBC: watch product switches and additional cardholders

RBC's offer terms focus on how you get the card. Typical wording excludes additional cardholders and existing RBC personal cardholders who transfer to the card during the offer period. The RBC Cash Back Mastercard's newcomer-page terms go further, excluding existing cardholders of any RBC personal credit card who apply for or transfer to that card.

The terms we reviewed for RBC's travel cards didn't state a fixed look-back period like TD's or CIBC's. That doesn't guarantee a bonus. Read the footnote for the exact offer you apply under, and ask RBC if you held the card recently.

## Aeroplan's own rule

Because Aeroplan is a separate program, Aeroplan co-branded cards carry two sets of rules: the issuer's and Aeroplan's. Aeroplan's general terms, as quoted in TD's offer terms, allow welcome points to be withheld or revoked if you previously received welcome or incentive points on a new Aeroplan card of the same category type, with any issuer. In practice, earning a bonus on a TD Aeroplan Visa Infinite and then on a CIBC Aeroplan Visa Infinite may not work the way you expect. See our [Aeroplan for beginners guide](/guides/aeroplan-for-beginners-canada/) for more on the program.

## Clawbacks and conditions after you qualify

Eligibility isn't the only fine print. Watch for:

- **Spending thresholds and deadlines.** Bonuses usually need a minimum spend within a set period. Returns reduce your eligible spend.
- **Staged bonuses.** Many offers pay part of the bonus in later months, or at your first anniversary.
- **Account in good standing.** A missed payment can disqualify you.
- **Early cancellation.** Some issuers can claw back bonus points if you close the card within a set period.
- **First-year fee waivers** that may apply only to the primary cardholder.

Never spend more than you otherwise would just to reach a threshold. Interest at typical purchase rates erases a welcome bonus quickly.

## A checklist before you apply

1. Open the card's offer page and read the eligibility footnote word for word.
2. List every card you've held with that issuer in the last two years, including as a supplementary cardholder, and the dates you opened or closed them.
3. For Aeroplan cards, note any Aeroplan card bonuses you've earned with any issuer.
4. Check the offer's application deadline.
5. Confirm you can meet the spending requirement with normal spending.
6. Screenshot the offer terms on the day you apply, in case you need to discuss a missing bonus later.

If a bonus doesn't arrive when expected, contact the issuer with your screenshot and account dates.

## FAQ

**Can I get the same welcome bonus twice in Canada?**
With American Express, the terms on most cards exclude current and former cardmembers of that card, so generally no. With TD and CIBC, the terms use a 12-month window; with Scotiabank, two years. Read the current terms for the specific card.

**Does product switching count as a new card for bonus purposes?**
Usually not. RBC, BMO, CIBC and Scotiabank terms exclude cardholders who switch from another of their cards. See our [product switching vs cancelling guide](/guides/product-switch-vs-cancel-credit-card-canada/).

**Does being an authorized user affect my eligibility?**
At Scotiabank it can: the two-year rule covers secondary cardholders. Other issuers' terms mostly focus on primary cardholders, but read the wording. Additional cardholders themselves generally aren't eligible for the primary's bonus.

**Why was I approved but didn't get the bonus?**
Approval decisions and bonus eligibility are separate. Issuers often state they "may approve" your application while you remain ineligible for the offer.

**Do welcome bonuses affect whether a card is worth keeping?**
They shouldn't. Judge a card on what it is worth in year two, after the bonus. Our [premium cards guide](/guides/are-premium-credit-cards-worth-it-canada/) shows how.
