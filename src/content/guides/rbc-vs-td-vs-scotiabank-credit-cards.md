**Choose the bank whose rewards currency matches trips and weekly spend you already have, not the bank with the flashiest welcome email.** RBC is the **Avion** shop — flexible travel points, WestJet cobrands, ION everyday earn, and a no-fee low-rate Visa. TD is the **Aeroplan** shop on Visa, plus First Class / Platinum travel, cash-back Visas, and a cheap USD Visa. Scotiabank is the **Scene+** shop, with Passport for no-FX travel, Momentum for grocery cash back, and American Express cards issued by Scotia if you want Amex inside the same login.

You can hold cards at more than one bank. Many people should. What you should not do is pay three Infinite fees for three currencies you will never combine. Start with one ecosystem, add a $0 card on the other network if tills require it, and use the [finder](/finder/) if the constraints are mixed.

Open the live lineups: [RBC](/issuer/rbc/), [TD](/issuer/td/), [Scotiabank](/issuer/scotiabank/). CIBC, BMO, and Amex are separate stories.

## If you already bank there

The honest reason to stay inside one bank is operations: one login, one relationship manager, possible fee waivers on Passport or Gold Amex when an eligible chequing package is attached, and simpler automatic payments.

The dishonest reason is loyalty for its own sake. A chequing account does not make Avion better than Aeroplan if you fly Air Canada every month.

- **RBC clients** who want travel without picking an airline should read Avion Infinite, then Platinum if income is short, then Privilege only if grocery/dining earn will use the extra rate. WestJet cobrands if that is the airline. [ION+](/card/rbc-ion-plus-visa/) if you want everyday Avion without a full travel-card fee story. [RateAdvantage](/card/rbc-rateadvantage-visa/) if you revolve.
- **TD clients** who fly Air Canada should compare [TD Aeroplan Visa Infinite](/card/td-aeroplan-visa-infinite-card/) with Privilege and with [Amex Aeroplan](/card/american-express-aeroplan-card/). TD wins Visa acceptance and grocery/gas categories; Amex often wins dining and FX treatment. If you do not fly AC, TD Cash Back or First Class Travel is the other door — do not buy Aeroplan as a generic points pile.
- **Scotia clients** must choose **Scene+ versus Momentum**. They are different products. Gold Amex and Scene+ Visa feed movies and Empire grocers. Momentum feeds cash back on grocery and recurring bills. Passport feeds FX-free travel and lounge passes. Holding Passport *and* Momentum *and* Gold Amex is three fees unless waivers make two of them cheap.

## Travel: Avion vs Aeroplan vs Scene+ / Passport

**Avion** is the “I do not know next year’s airline” currency. Redemption through RBC or as a statement credit can be worth less than a partner transfer. Base earn on Infinite is ordinary. You are paying for flexibility and the welcome path. Privilege is the grocery/dining bump at a Privilege fee and income bar.

**Aeroplan** is a frequent-flyer program. It is excellent when you understand award seats and poor when you only buy WestJet seat sales. TD’s cobrand ladder (Platinum → Infinite → Privilege) is the Visa way to feed it. CIBC and Amex also sell Aeroplan cards if TD is the wrong bank.

**Scene+** is not Aeroplan. Passport is a travel *card* (no FX, lounge passes) that happens to earn Scene+. Hotel and car bookings through Scene+ Travel can earn extra. Everyday 6x grocery Scene+ lives on [Gold American Express](/card/scotiabank-gold-american-express-card/), not on Passport.

If you want a longer travel decision tree, use [how to choose a travel card](/guides/how-to-choose-travel-credit-card-canada/).

## Everyday spend: ION vs TD Cash Back vs Momentum / Scene+

RBC’s ION cards are everyday Avion earners — useful if you want one currency in the house. They are not 4% cash back.

TD Cash Back Visas are simple statement-credit products, including a $0 version in our data. They are the TD answer if you pay in full and do not want Aeroplan.

Scotia Momentum Visa Infinite is the 4%-class grocery-and-bills cash-back Infinite in our review, with a first-year waiver on some offers and a short 10% welcome — and a two-year Scotia-card exclusion. Scene+ Visa is the $0 points alternative at Empire banners.

Pick cash or points. Mixing Momentum and Gold Amex only makes sense if grocery is huge *and* you redeem Scene+ elsewhere. Most households need one.

## USD, students, and low interest

All three banks sell a USD card. TD’s is the no-rewards cheap Visa; RBC’s earns Avion; Scotia’s is plain. See the [USD guide](/guides/best-us-dollar-credit-cards-canada/).

Scotia has the thicker **student** shelf in our catalog (Scene+, Momentum, Amex). RBC and TD students often land on adult $0 cards. See the [student guide](/guides/best-student-credit-cards-canada/).

Low interest: TD Low Rate (purchase teaser), RBC RateAdvantage (prime-plus, $0), Scotia Value Visa. See the [low-interest guide](/guides/low-interest-vs-rewards-credit-cards-canada/).

## Cross-bank pairs that are sane

- **TD Aeroplan Infinite + Tangerine Mastercard** — Visa Aeroplan plus a $0 Mastercard for Costco and Amex-less tills.
- **Avion Infinite + $0 Scene+ or Tangerine** — flexible travel plus a grocery/network backup.
- **Passport + Tangerine or Dividend** — no-FX travel plus real grocery cash if Passport’s everyday earn is weak.
- **One paid card + one $0 card at the same bank** only if the $0 card is actually $0 and you will use both.

Insane: Avion Privilege + Aeroplan Privilege + Passport Privilege. That is a lounge collection, not a wallet.

## Common mistakes

- Applying at all three banks in one week for “the best bonus.” Three inquiries, three exclusion clocks.
- Buying Aeroplan because a colleague likes Air Canada, then flying WestJet.
- Paying for Passport without foreign-currency spend.
- Ignoring Scotia’s two-year rule and counting on a welcome offer you will not get.
- Staying in a bank’s cards because the chequing app is familiar while the currency is wrong.
